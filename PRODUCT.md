# Product

<!-- impeccable:product-schema 1 -->

## Platform

web

## Stack

Static HTML/CSS/JS, no build step and no framework. A single self-contained `index.html` (inline CSS and JS, hand-drawn SVG, no third-party chart library) plus separate data files. Everything must run from `file://` by double-click on a machine with no toolchain and possibly no network, so data ships as classic scripts attached to `window.AFFINITY_DATA` rather than ES modules. The deliverable is a zip (`.js` attachments get stripped by mail filters).

## Users

One primary viewer: a senior global leader inside the company, non-technical and with no data background, who has very little time. She will see this once, briefly, likely projected or over a shoulder. Success is that within the first seconds she is visually impressed, understands that the AI and the human panel agree, and remembers the project afterwards.

The presenter (the author of this dashboard) drives the screen and talks over it; the viewer does not explore it herself.

## Product Purpose

A one-screen demonstration that a DINOv3-based vision model can predict sensory panel scores for foundation from photographs of the product worn on a face, closely enough to be trusted. It exists to win continued investment in the project — not to be a working analysis tool.

Success: the viewer leaves believing "the AI agrees with the panel, and this is worth funding." Failure modes are looking generic or machine-made, requiring explanation, or taking more than a minute to land.

## Positioning

The claim is agreement with an existing, trusted human instrument (the Optiscreen sensory panel), not replacement of it. The dashboard's argument is always AI-versus-panel side by side, never the AI's numbers alone.

## Operating Context

- Built on macOS; **presented from a Windows work ThinkPad**, possibly offline and behind a corporate network. Rendering must be judged on Windows.
- Single fixed viewport: 1920 × 1050, no scrolling. Everything is on one screen at once.
- Viewing is a live talk-through, once, under time pressure. There is no onboarding, no help, no second session.
- Fonts and every asset are self-hosted (OFL `.woff2`); no runtime CDN or Google Fonts dependency.

## Capabilities and Constraints

- Domain model: formulas × 3 wear time points (T0 / T3 / T6) × 8 fixed sensory attributes × 2 values (panel score, AI score), on a 0–6 scale to one decimal.
- The 8 attributes and the 0–6 scale are fixed by the sensory team and cannot be renamed or rescaled.
- Metric terminology, as chosen by the user: **Acceptance Rate** (`|ai − panel| ≤ 0.5` counts as accepted) — never called "accuracy"; **Average Difference** — never displayed as "MAE".
- Data is **mock**, generated by `scripts/generate-data.mjs` from a seeded, constraint-checked search, calibrated against one real Lancome TIU chart. Only three formulas are mocked (NARS LightR, LANCOME TIU, YSL All Hours). The spec's wider counts (53 formulas, 1272 pairs) are aspirational copy, not backed by data in this repo — a known, accepted inconsistency.
- One-off deliverable: no error states, empty states, loading states, multi-user concerns, responsive breakpoints, or long-term maintenance are in scope.
- Product photography does not exist yet; a bottle silhouette placeholder stands in, to be swapped by the sensory team later.

## Brand Commitments

- Title: **Foundation Affinity × AI-lization**. Subtitle: **AI-model powered by DINOv3 vs. Optiscreen Panel**.
- The desaturated purple is the company's fixed presentation brand color and cannot be changed.
- The binding overriding constraint from the user: **no AI slop**. Choices that read as machine defaults (generic fonts, stock editorial layout tropes) are treated as failures even when otherwise defensible. The user selects visual options themselves from side-by-side specimens rather than having them chosen for them.
- **"No AI slop" is not a mandate for restraint.** Stripping the design bare to avoid looking generic is the opposite failure and is equally unacceptable: the user asked for a screen that looks *fancy*, and under-design fails that outright. Decorative elements, effects, and ornament are permitted and wanted wherever they earn their place, so long as they do not seriously damage the overall vibe. The judgment call is "is this distinctive and considered," never "is this the safest amount of design."
- `DESIGN.md` in this repository is the authoritative visual specification, written by the user. It governs; it is not to be replaced or freelanced away from.

## Evidence on Hand

- `DESIGN.md` — the user-authored visual and interaction spec.
- `data-core.js`, `data-nars-lightr.js`, `data-lancome-tiu.js`, `data-ysl-all-hours.js` — generated mock data on `window.AFFINITY_DATA`.
- `scripts/generate-data.mjs` — the generator plus its verification output (global acceptance rate, per-attribute, per-formula, T0-vs-T6 comparison).
- `Screenshot 2026-09-18 at 00.52.36.png` — one real Lancome TIU panel chart, the trend reference for the mock data.
- `specimens/didone.html`, `specimens/type-pairing.html` — typeface comparison pages the user chose from.
- **Absent, must not be fabricated:** real model performance figures, real product photography, real data for any formula beyond the three mocked, and the "~70% time saved" figure (an explicit demo placeholder the sensory scientists will replace).

## Product Principles

1. **Agreement is the whole argument.** Every element either shows the AI matching the panel or gets cut.
2. **One glance, then the talk.** The screen must land its point before the presenter says anything; detail rewards attention but is never required.
3. **Nothing invented.** Placeholder numbers stay labelled as placeholders; missing data is never filled in with plausible-looking figures.
4. **Windows-offline or it does not exist.** A choice that only renders on the build machine is not a choice.
5. **Distinctive or it is not persuasive.** For this viewer, looking machine-generated undercuts the credibility of the result itself.
6. **Under-designed is also a failure.** Avoiding generic never means avoiding effort. The target is "fancy and considered," reached by making richer, more specific choices — not by removing until nothing can be criticized.
